How JavaScript trimStart and trimEnd Handle Whitespace

This article covers the behavior of JavaScript's String.prototype.trimStart() and String.prototype.trimEnd() methods, specifically focusing on how they process whitespace characters. You will learn which characters ECMAScript defines as whitespace, how these methods identify and strip leading or trailing characters, and how internal whitespace remains unaffected.

What Are trimStart() and trimEnd()?

Introduced in ECMAScript 2019, trimStart() and trimEnd() are built-in string methods used to eliminate whitespace from the extremities of a string:

  • trimStart() removes whitespace from the beginning (left side) of a string. It is also aliased by trimLeft().
  • trimEnd() removes whitespace from the end (right side) of a string. It is also aliased by trimRight().

Both methods return a new string. Because JavaScript strings are immutable, the original string remains untouched.

const text = "   Hello World!   ";

console.log(text.trimStart()); // "Hello World!   "
console.log(text.trimEnd());   // "   Hello World!"

Characters Recognized as Whitespace

These methods do not just remove the standard space bar character (U+0020). Under the ECMAScript specification, they target three broad categories of characters:

1. Standard Whitespace Characters

  • Space (\u0020)
  • Character tabulation / Tab (\t or \u0009)
  • Line tabulation / Vertical tab (\v or \u000B)
  • Form feed (\f or \u000C)
  • No-break space (\u00A0)
  • Zero-width no-break space / Byte Order Mark (\uFEFF)

2. Line Terminator Characters

  • Line feed / Newline (\n or \u000A)
  • Carriage return (\r or \u000D)
  • Line separator (\u2028)
  • Paragraph separator (\u2029)

3. Unicode "Separator, Space" (Zs) Category

Any character categorized under the Unicode Zs general category is treated as whitespace and removed. This includes:

  • En quad (\u2000) and Em quad (\u2001)
  • En space (\u2002) and Em space (\u2003)
  • Three-per-em space (\u2004), Four-per-em space (\u2005), and Six-per-em space (\u2006)
  • Figure space (\u2007) and Punctuation space (\u2008)
  • Thin space (\u2009) and Hair space (\u200A)
  • Narrow no-break space (\u202F)
  • Medium mathematical space (\u205F)
  • Ideographic space (\u3000)

Behavior on Mixed Whitespace

When multiple, consecutive whitespace characters of different types appear at the target boundary, trimStart() and trimEnd() consume all of them until a non-whitespace character is encountered.

// A mix of tabs, newlines, standard spaces, and Unicode spaces
const mixed = "\t\n \u3000Data Entry\u00A0\r\n";

console.log(JSON.stringify(mixed.trimStart()));
// Output: "\"Data Entry\\u00a0\\r\\n\""

console.log(JSON.stringify(mixed.trimEnd()));
// Output: "\"\\t\\n \\u3000Data Entry\""

Important Exceptions: What Is Not Trimmed

A common source of confusion is the Zero-Width Space (\u200B). Although its name includes "space," it belongs to the Unicode Cf (Format) category, not Zs. As a result, neither trimStart() nor trimEnd() will remove it.

const zeroWidth = "\u200BText";
console.log(zeroWidth.trimStart() === zeroWidth); // true (not removed)

Furthermore, neither method removes whitespace located inside the string; all characters situated between the first and last non-whitespace characters are preserved.
